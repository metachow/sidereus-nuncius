# Review: English p023–p055 (part3.yaml p023–p028, f16ar.2, f16br.1; part4.yaml p029–p055)

Checks: part3 "OK, no problems" (44 entries); part4 "OK, no problems" (94 entries, f17v running long).

## Changes, part3
- p023.01: "I did not notice it" → "We observed it ... and showed it" (Latin *a nobis observata*; "I" kept for *duxi*).
- p024.01: "field of the sky" → "field of the ether" (*aetheris*; matches p028.01).
- p024.04: "after the first quarter" → "after the first quadrature" (Latin *quadraturam*; matches the footnote and p024.12).
- p024.13: *quid mirum? maxime:* was "Why is that surprising? Indeed it is not", which contradicts *maxime*; now "What is so strange in that? Just so: ...". Also *ipsum lunare corpus*, "opaque and dark".
- p024.19: "a quarter of the way removed" → "removed from the Sun by a quadrant" (*per quadrantem*).
- p024.21: "least illuminated" → "in turn ... less illuminated" (*minus vice versa*).
- p024.22: "company of the stars" → "dance of the stars" (*chorea*).
- p025.03: "crowned with twinkling rays" → "wearing twinkling rays like hair" (*crinita*; sets up "borrowed locks" and p025.06 "unshorn / shave off").
- p026.02: awkward "with a like form with the spyglass and with natural sight" rephrased.
- p028.01: *specillum* "lens" → "glass" (the whole instrument is meant here; see question 2).

## Changes, part4
- Log formulas brought into line with the rulings throughout: every "min."/"sec." written out ("3 minutes", "0 minutes 40 seconds", "4 minutes 30 seconds", "1 minute 20 seconds"). Cardinal hours: "at hour 0 and 30 minutes", "at hour 2", "at hour 1 after sunset". "After sunset" appears only where the Latin has *ab occasu* (p040.01, p041.01, p046.01, p050.01); p043.01, p044.01, p048.01, p049.01 are without it, as in the Latin. Ordinal hours: "at the fifth hour"; "of the night" only where the Latin has *noctis*.
- p035.01: "at the first hour of the night" → "of the following night" (*hora sequentis noctis prima*).
- Dates made consistent: "On the 7th of January", "on the 8th", "On the 1st of February" (were spelled out).
- p031.01–03, p032.04: person restored to Galileo's "we" (*enarravimus, aperiamus, convocantes, nobis licuit, monitos facimus, descripsimus, diximus*). The English had "I".
- p032.01: "(which had not been possible before ...)" → "(this had not happened before ...)" (*contigerat*); "I saw" → "I perceived" (*cognovi*). Italian-hours footnote corrected (see doubts).
- p032.06: retrograde footnote reworded: Jupiter really was retrograde, not just "by the tables".
- p033.04: "with more care and scruple" → "more attentively and scrupulously".
- p034.03: "differences of hours can be taken" → "hour-to-hour differences can usually be perceived".
- p036.02, p038.02: *enim* rendered as "for" (was "though" / dropped).
- p037.01: "The 14th was cloudy weather" → "On the 14th the weather was cloudy".
- p038.01: "arrangement just drawn" → "drawn here" (*proxime depicta*: the figure follows).
- p038.06: "hardly more than 30 sec." → "barely 30 seconds" (*vix 30 tantum*; the old wording got the sense wrong).
- p040.05: Latin *min. tantum sec. 20* (no minute figure) → "only 20 seconds" (as the Chinese edition has it).
- p029.01, p030.01: \tremph{Nebula} of Orion's Head / \tremph{Nebula} called \tremph{Praesepe} (glossary form; capsemph covers *nebulosam Praesepe*). Footnotes: Collinder~69 "in the head of Orion", M42 "in the Sword"; M44 "in Cancer"; the Aselli γ and δ Cnc "flank it to the north and south".
- p047.02: "moderately" → "slightly" (*modice*). p047.04, p049.03 footnotes tightened ("probably" instead of "must be").
- Superlatives that render Latin comparatives (*occidentalior/occidentalius*) changed to "the more western".
- Straight apostrophes → ’.

## Resolved doubts (translators' notes)
- en_part3 p026.02 *Canem*: kept "the Dog" with the Sirius footnote. Sirius = α CMa, brightest star of the night sky: correct.
- en_part3 p025.06 "shave off": kept; p025.03 now sets up the hair metaphor.
- en_part3 p024.13: syntax resolved as above (*maxime* = emphatic assent).
- en_part3 p028.02 \tremph{nebulous}: correct, Latin has \capsemph.
- en_part4 p031.02: recast closer to the Latin (*occasionem reperiendi atque observandi* = "the occasion of their discovery and observation").
- en_part4 p032.06 *secus a computo astronomico directus*: "moving direct, contrary to the computation" is right. Jupiter was retrograde (opposition about December 1609, station about February 1610). Direct (eastward) motion would have put it east of the stars, which is Galileo's worry.
- en_part4 p039.03: comparison with the remote star, correct as is.
- en_part4 p045.02 *minutorum primorum 5*: "5 minutes", fine.
- "0 hours 30 minutes after sunset" glossary decision in en_part4 replaced by the editorial ruling.
